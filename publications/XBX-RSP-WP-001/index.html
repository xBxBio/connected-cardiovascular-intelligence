Abstract

Cardiovascular care generates increasingly complex and heterogeneous information across electrocardiographic waveforms, cardiovascular imaging, hemodynamics, laboratory measurements, medications, clinical observations, symptoms, genomic and molecular information, wearable and implantable devices, remote physiologic monitoring, procedures, clinical narratives, and patient-reported data. Although these information sources may individually contribute to clinical assessment, fragmentation across systems, formats, institutions, modalities, and time can limit the ability to construct a coherent representation of a patient's evolving cardiovascular state.

This white paper introduces Connected Cardiovascular Intelligence (CCI), a clinician-centered, longitudinal, multimodal framework for integrating and interpreting cardiovascular information while preserving temporal relationships, clinical context, provenance, data quality, intervention history, uncertainty, and human clinical oversight. Rather than representing cardiovascular health primarily as a collection of independent observations or episodic snapshots, CCI conceptualizes the patient as an evolving clinical trajectory in which measurements, interventions, physiologic responses, and outcomes contribute to a continuously updated representation of cardiovascular state.

The proposed xBxBio CCI reference framework comprises interconnected layers encompassing patient and clinical context, multimodal data acquisition, interoperability and semantic normalization, data quality and provenance, temporal and event architecture, longitudinal patient-state representation, computational intelligence, clinical decision support, human clinical oversight, and outcome feedback. Quality management, validation, regulatory governance, privacy, cybersecurity, data governance, and lifecycle monitoring operate as cross-cutting controls across the framework. Artificial intelligence and machine learning are positioned as computational components within this broader architecture rather than as independent clinical authorities.
